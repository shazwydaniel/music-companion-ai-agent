## Major and minor triads

**Source ID:** `major-minor-triads`  
**Topic:** Chord construction  
**Instruments:** Guitar, piano

A triad contains a root, a third, and a fifth. A major triad has a major third and perfect fifth above the root. A minor triad has a minor third and perfect fifth. In semitones above the root, the formulas are 0–4–7 for major and 0–3–7 for minor.

C major uses C, E, and G. C minor uses C, E-flat, and G. To hear the difference on a keyboard, play C major, lower the E by one semitone, and play the new chord. The root and fifth stay in place.

## I–IV–V progressions

**Source ID:** `one-four-five-progressions`  
**Topic:** Chord progressions  
**Instruments:** Guitar, piano

Roman numerals identify chords by their root's scale degree. In C major, I is C major, IV is F major, and V is G major. A simple four-bar exercise is C, F, G, C. Play one chord per bar. Listen to the return from G to C.

To move the same exercise to G major, find the first, fourth, and fifth scale degrees in G. The new progression is G, C, D, G. The relationship between the chords stays the same.

## Keyboard inversions

**Source ID:** `keyboard-inversions`  
**Topic:** Piano voicings  
**Instruments:** Piano

In root position, the root is the lowest note. A C major triad in root position can be played as C–E–G. In first inversion, E is lowest and the chord can be played as E–G–C. In second inversion, G is lowest and the chord can be played as G–C–E. All three contain the same notes.

Practice by playing root position, then moving the lowest note up an octave. Repeat to reach second inversion. Compare how each version feels under the hand before using it in a progression.

## Practicing clean guitar chord changes

**Source ID:** `clean-guitar-changes`  
**Topic:** Beginner guitar practice  
**Instruments:** Guitar

Choose two chords you already know, such as G major and C major. Form the first chord and pluck each string you intend to play. If a note is muted or buzzing, adjust finger placement and test it again. Repeat for the second chord.

Set a comfortable slow tempo and give yourself four beats per chord. Alternate between the two shapes for one minute. Keep the pulse steady and pause to fix any consistently muted notes. Try two beats per chord once the transitions are comfortable.
